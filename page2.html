❌ Option A: Approved Without Flagging

  • Immediate Meeting Workflow passes unchallenged; nobody names the missing AI disclosure out loud.
  • 3 Months Post-Rollout Patients feel blindsided by unexplained calls; frontline clinicians escalate complaints after harm has occurred.
  • Coordinator Role Outcome Seen as a passive checklist follower; senior staff take over oversight duties while engineering rebuilds the workflow.

✅ Option B: Named & Routed Risk

  • Immediate Meeting Coordinator names the consent-boundary concern; Senior PM halts rollout for Ethics Committee review.
  • 3 Months Post-Rollout Workflow launches with transparent AI disclosure; patients make informed choices with zero complaints.
  • Coordinator Role Outcome Earns senior leadership trust for proactive oversight—without needing a philosophy degree or making policy alone.
Key Takeaway: Entry-level ethics fluency does not mean making final ethics policy judgments—those stay with clinical leaders and ethics committees. It means recognizing when a routine scenario touches a core concept, naming it precisely, and routing it to the people who can act before patients are affected.

Reflection Takeaway

Notice that distinguishing yourself as a trusted coordinator does not require a background in bioethics or the authority to rewrite clinical policy alone. By building a working habit of checking routine AI workflows against core concepts like consent boundaries and patient autonomy, you catch hidden risks early and route them to decision-makers before patients are harmed.
